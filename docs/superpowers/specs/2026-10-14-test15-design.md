# Test 15 — fixes and point shaping before the phone tests (2026-10-14)

Kerem checked Test 14 (branch `samples-6b`) in the browser and asked for five things before he tests on iOS and
Android, all three platforms (they share the core). His answers to the design questions are quoted; the rest are my
calls, written here so they can be argued with.

## 1. A stretch point at level 0 keeps sounding (bug)

Kerem: it is the Stretch point's *level*. **Not reproduced.** Measured on this branch and on the live site, in the
core (native test) and in the page (headless Chrome, a real knob drag, soloed and not, route voices on and off): the
point falls to silence in about 4 s (the bed's 350 ms fade). What keeps sounding at about -20 dB beside his two
published stretch points (both saved at level 0) is Koşuyolu's route voices, two of them samplers playing field
recordings (leaves, a stream) that can sound like a stretch.

Done anyway, because it is a real defect found on the way: a point at level 0 is no longer *picked* by the engine.
Before, it held one of the few bed voices (so a nearer point that sounds could not play) and ran its FFTs for
nothing. Kerem is asked which browser and what he did, to find the case I could not.

## 5. A rhythm part vanishes when its drive and crush go up (bug, fixed)

Root cause, measured: the crusher rounds to steps of 0.5^(bits-1) of *full scale*; at full crush (2 bits) a step is
0.5, so a hit whose samples stay under 0.25 rounds to exact silence. Field recordings are quiet: a hit peaking at
0.25 fell to -62.7 dB, one at 0.08 to -245 dB. Drive's make-up (0.6 / (1 + 3.4 drive)) left a driven hit 17 dB down
at the peak, so crush + drive cost even a loud hit 10 dB.

Fix: both run at the hit's own scale (its recording's peak at this hit's level); the bits follow the knob across
its whole travel (2 + 10 (1 - crush)^1.5; before, nothing audible changed below 0.75); drive's make-up 1 / (1 + 2
drive). After: no hit vanishes at any recording level; drive full is about +4 dB (it saturates), crush + drive about
-3.6 dB.

## 2. EQ and note envelopes on stretch, rhythm and grains points

Kerem: "per event". The EQ is the route voices' five-band graph; one per point.

- **Rhythm (hits):** `rhythm.env` = [attack s, decay s, sustain, release s] on every hit; the gate is one step of
  the pattern, then the release, exponential to silence (A-3). Absent: the hit plays as now. One envelope and one
  EQ per point, for all four parts (call: four graphs would not fit one panel).
- **Grains:** the existing grain envelope (attack, decay, sustain, release as fractions of the grain) gets the same
  draggable graph in place of its four sliders. Same data, same sound.
- **Stretch:** `sound.env` shapes how the recording's pitch arrives on each chord change: the old chord's tuned
  partials release, then the new chord's bloom in (attack, decay to sustain). It scales the pitched part only
  (tune, resonate, low end, sing); the stretch underneath keeps going. Sequential, not overlapping (one voice cannot
  hold two chords): the chord switches when the release ends. Absent: the chord glides as now.
- EQ: `rhythm.eq` / `sound.eq` = [[Hz, dB, Q] x5], the route voices' format. A flat EQ is the input bit for bit.

## 3. Pitch from non-pitched recordings in the stretch

Kerem: "both as a blend and adjustable". Two new shape keys, both under Harmony:

- `sing` 0..1 — how far the recording becomes pitched (0 = off, bit-identical to now).
- `contour` 0..1 — between **body** (0) and **shape** (1):
  - body: every chord note in every octave with its overtones, each partial as loud as the recording is at that
    frequency right now (its colour, smoothed over a third of an octave). Rich and steady.
  - shape: each frame the recording's strongest spectral peaks (a whistle, a hum, a wind tone; at most 8, each well
    above its surroundings) are moved onto the nearest chord tone in their own octave, with overtones weighted by
    the recording's colour. The pitches rise and fall as the recording does. A peak keeps its chord tone while it
    stays within 80 cents of where it was (hysteresis, A-9), so it does not flicker between two notes.
- Both are painted as magnitudes before the random phases (the stretch stays last), energy-matched to the frame so
  the level does not move (A-18). Focus sets the partials' width, Partials how many.

## 4. Grains in the route's key

Kerem: "each grain's own peak". A grains point's recording gets a peak track in the core (one 2048-point frame
every 1024 samples, worked through one frame per audio block after it loads, so nothing stalls). When `follow
chord` is up and the recording is unpitched (no YIN track), each grain is retuned from its own place's dominant
peak to the nearest chord note. Grains from different places land on different chord tones. Pitched recordings keep
the YIN track as now. Hits are unchanged (scope).

## Platforms

The rhythm and grains changes and Sing reach every platform through the core (the rhythm JSON and the shape keys go
straight in). The stretch point's EQ and envelope are one shared call, `fs_point_sound` (the web engine, `ios/Walk.swift`,
Android's `Walk.kt` + `jni.cpp`), and both apps' walks no longer give a level-0 point a voice.

  envelope: sustain 0 leaves the plain stretch (16%), sustain 1 the tuned (28%); to E major, 0.2 s after the change the old
