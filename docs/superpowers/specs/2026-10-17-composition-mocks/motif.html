The motif editor, and how it develops

A 4-note idea over one bar. The rows are what you write in; that choice decides whether the motif bends to every chord or keeps its own notes.

9 7 5 3 root
over C maj7: C — G — E — D  ·  over A m9: A — E — C — B

A · Chord degrees

Rows are the chord's own tones (root, 3, 5, 7, 9). The motif always fits: it re-voices itself on every chord. Its melody changes with the harmony, so it is recognisable by rhythm and shape more than by its exact notes.

B · Scale degrees of the route's key

Rows are the key's scale; the current chord's tones are lit as a guide. The motif keeps its exact melody on every chord (a real tune you'd hum); the chords colour it, and some notes rub against some chords.

C · Note tokens

A row of small tokens instead of a grid: degree and length per note, typed or nudged with arrows. Very compact and precise; you don't see the rhythm as a picture.

Underneath any of them: the development chain

A chords 1–2→ A ↑2 3–4→ A inverted 5–6→ fragment ½ ×2 7–8→ cadence → tonic 16 + step
each chip is one operation (repeat · sequence ↑↓N · invert · retrograde · augment · diminish · fragment · cadence), dragged to reorder, its span shown on the chord timeline above.

My recommendation: A (chord degrees) as the default, with a switch on the motif to B when you want a fixed tune. The chain works the same for both.
